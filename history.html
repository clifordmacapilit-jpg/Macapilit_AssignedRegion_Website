Our History & Culture

The story of Northern Mindanao

A Brief History

Long before Spanish arrival, Indigenous groups such as the Higaonon, Talaandig and Subanen lived in the highlands and river valleys of what is now Northern Mindanao. Coastal settlements traded with neighbors across the Mindanao and Bohol seas.

Spanish missionaries arrived in the 1600s and founded settlements such as Cagayan de Misamis. In 1871 a volcanic eruption on Camiguin destroyed the old town of Catarman and sank part of the island. The Sunken Cemetery marks the spot today.

In modern times, the region grew into an industrial and agricultural center. Cagayan de Oro became a hub for trade, Iligan became known for steel and hydropower, and Bukidnon became one of the country's main producers of pineapple and corn.

Cultural Highlights

Festivals

  • Kagay-an Festival (Cagayan de Oro, August)
  • Lanzones Festival (Camiguin, October)
  • Kaamulan Festival (Bukidnon, Feb-March)
  • Diyandi Festival (Iligan, September)
  • Tabakuhan Festival (Misamis Oriental)

Traditional Crafts & Foods

  • Higaonon and Talaandig woven baskets and beadwork
  • Subanen embroidered textiles
  • Pastel (soft bread filled with yema), Camiguin
  • Lanzones and pineapple
  • Satti and Tuna dishes
